From Oddanchatram, Kodaikanal looks close on the map: the Palani Hills rise right behind the town. The road, though, is the part that decides how your day goes. **Oddanchatram to Kodaikanal travel** for a group means a long stretch of hill road, a town that fills up on weekends and a return journey that you want to finish in daylight. This guide helps you plan the day — distance, timing, a sensible itinerary and how to keep the whole group together, including in a **Force Urbania** arranged by **OG Tours and Travels**.

*Distances and routes are approximate and based on public sources that vary. Please verify your route on a map. We have deliberately not quoted journey times; they change with traffic, weather and the day.*

## How far is Kodaikanal from Oddanchatram, and which way do we go?

The distance is in the region of **90 km**. The usual way to think of it is Oddanchatram to Palani — roughly 28 km — and then from the Palani side up the ghat towards Kodaikanal, which public sources put at around 65–70 km. The hill section has sharp bends and gradients, so it takes longer than kilometres alone suggest. Check the current route on a map and look at road and weather updates on the day.

If you are also planning to visit the Palani temple, read the [Oddanchatram to Palani and Kodaikanal group trip guide](/blog/oddanchatram-palani-kodaikanal-group-trip), which treats the two as one trip.

## What is the best time of day and year to go?

**Time of day:** leave early. You reach Kodaikanal before the day-trippers and before afternoon mist, and you have the best chance of returning before dark.

**Season:** winter (roughly December to February) is cool and clear but cold in the evenings; summer (roughly March to June) is popular and crowded; the monsoon (roughly June to September) is green but wet, and rain and mist slow the hill road. Sources vary on exact months, so check conditions when you plan.

## What does a sensible group day plan look like?

A relaxed plan for a one-day trip from Oddanchatram:

1. **Early morning departure** from your agreed pickup point.
2. **A break on the way up** — tea, a restroom stop, a pause for anyone feeling unwell.
3. **Late morning: Kodai Lake or Coaker's Walk** — gentle, easy for all ages.
4. **Lunch in town**, rested, before the afternoon crowds peak.
5. **One or two viewpoints** — for example Pillar Rocks or Green Valley View — depending on weather and energy.
6. **Leave by mid-afternoon** to be back down in daylight.

A one-day trip is tiring for older travellers and young children; a night in Kodaikanal is gentler. ## What should the group carry?

- A **light jacket** for everyone — it is cooler and damper than at the foothills.
- **Water, snacks, wet wipes and plastic bags** for the ghat.
- **Motion-sickness medicine** and a change of clothes for children.
- A small **umbrella or raincoat**.
- **ID proof** and a phone with enough charge; signal can be weak on stretches of the hill road.

## Hill-road tips for groups

- **Avoid the ghat in heavy rain or after dark** if possible.
- **Take a break rather than pressing on** if someone feels unwell.
- **Seat those who feel motion sickness towards the front.**
- **Allow buffer time on weekends and holidays**, when traffic into town slows down.

## What is a good trip plan if we want an overnight stay?

If you decide to stay the night, the trip becomes easier on everyone:

- **Day one:** leave after an early breakfast, break the climb, reach Kodaikanal before lunch, keep the afternoon light — the lake, a park, an early dinner.
- **Day two:** a calm morning, one or two viewpoints while the air is clear, then lunch and the return in daylight.

An overnight stay also lets you avoid the weekend crowds by timing your arrival and departure against the rush. Book accommodation ahead on weekends and in school holidays; we do not list hotels in this guide.

## Special situations from Oddanchatram

- **Combining a Palani temple visit.** Many families from this area want both. Do not squeeze them into one day if older members are travelling; treat Palani as one half-day and Kodaikanal as another day.
- **Travelling with elderly relatives.** Tell the operator in advance, plan more breaks and avoid a dense itinerary. Seat them where entering and leaving the vehicle is easiest.
- **Travelling in the rainy season.** Mist and rain slow the hill road and hide views. Keep the plan flexible and decide on the morning of the trip if conditions look poor.
- **A function or reunion.** If you are travelling for a family event, plan the vehicle times around the event, not the other way round. Our notes on [group vehicles for weddings and temple visits](/blog/group-vehicle-weddings-temple-visits-dindigul-district) cover this.
