## What is the usual route from Dindigul to Kodaikanal?

Public sources describe the common road as Dindigul → Batlagundu (Vathalagundu) → the ghat road up to Kodaikanal, signed as State Highway 156. Batlagundu is roughly 35 km from Dindigul by some accounts, and the climb begins a short distance beyond it. The whole distance is quoted anywhere from about 75 km to about 100 km. Treat these as a range and check your exact pickup point on a map.

The key practical point: a large part of the trip is a **ghat road with hairpin bends**, so it takes longer than the kilometres imply. Our [Kodaikanal ghat road tips for group travel](/blog/kodaikanal-ghat-road-tips-for-group-travel) covers how to make that stretch comfortable.

## What should I agree with the operator before the trip?

### Pickup and drop
Give the exact pickup landmark in Dindigul and say whether you want to be dropped back at the same place. If relatives join from different parts of town, list each stop and the time.

### Which places in Kodaikanal
Tell the operator your main stops so the day is realistic. Most families choose a few from Kodai Lake, Coaker's Walk, Pillar Rocks, Green Valley View and Bryant Park. Our [guide to the best places to visit in Kodaikanal](/blog/best-places-to-visit-in-kodaikanal-with-family) sorts them by effort.

### Return time
Agree the time you want to start down. Descending in daylight is easier on everyone. Mist and evening traffic can slow the return.

### What is included
Fuel, driver's allowances, tolls, parking and any entry points on the way are items to name in the quotation. Parking in Kodaikanal gets tight at weekends and holidays, which is a good reason to ask who handles it.

## When should we leave Dindigul?

Early, and choose the season with care — [the best time to visit Kodaikanal from Dindigul](/blog/best-time-to-visit-kodaikanal-from-dindigul) compares winter, summer and the monsoon. A morning start means you are on the hill road while it is cooler and before the main stream of weekend traffic, you reach the lake before the crowd and you leave time for lunch. If you cannot start early, consider an overnight stay and read [how to plan a comfortable weekend trip to Kodaikanal](/blog/comfortable-weekend-trip-to-kodaikanal) or the [two-day plan from Dindigul](/blog/two-day-kodaikanal-trip-from-dindigul).

## What should we carry?

- Light jackets or sweaters for everyone, children included.
- Water, snacks, wet wipes and some plastic bags.
- Motion-sickness remedies and a spare set of clothes for small children.
- ID proof for adults and a charged phone. Signal can drop on parts of the hill.
- A small umbrella or raincoat.

## Can we visit Dindigul sights on the way?

A short stop is possible, but resist the temptation. Starting a Kodaikanal day with a long Rock Fort climb leaves less time and energy for the hill. If you want both, spread them across two days; our [Dindigul sightseeing guide](/blog/dindigul-sightseeing-group-tour) lists what to see in town.
