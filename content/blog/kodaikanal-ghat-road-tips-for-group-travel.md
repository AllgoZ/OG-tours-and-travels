For groups from Dindigul, the hardest part of a Kodaikanal trip is not Kodaikanal. It is the **ghat road**: the winding climb that takes the family from the plains to the hill station. Most people cope perfectly well — but a bit of preparation turns an unsettling climb into a pleasant one, and it matters most when children, older relatives or a nervous traveller are along. These **Kodaikanal ghat road tips** are for passengers, organisers and anyone planning a trip with **OG Tours and Travels**.

*Sources disagree on the length of the ghat section and the number of bends, so we give none. The commonly described route goes from Dindigul via Batlagundu, and the road is State Highway 156. Check conditions before you leave.*

## What is the ghat road like?

The climb begins a short way beyond Batlagundu and winds up the hillside with hairpin bends and narrow stretches. Visibility changes with cloud, and the temperature drops as you gain height. There are viewpoints and places to stop, but not everywhere. The road deserves respect, and the driver's job is to drive it steadily, not quickly.

## How do I prepare before the climb?

- **Top up fuel in Dindigul or Batlagundu.** Public sources note there is no petrol bunk on the ghat stretch.
- **Eat lightly.** Heavy, oily food before winding roads makes motion sickness worse.
- **Use the restroom before the climb starts.** Decide stops in advance.
- **Keep water and snacks within reach**, not in the luggage compartment.
- **Check the weather.** If the forecast shows heavy rain or low cloud, consider postponing.

## How do I manage motion sickness?

- **Seat anyone prone to it near the front** and looking ahead.
- **Keep fresh air flowing** where the weather allows.
- **Avoid screens, books and phones** on the climb.
- **Carry remedies** recommended by your doctor, and give them before the climb, not after it starts.
- **Keep plastic bags and wet wipes** within reach.
- **Take a break** at the first sign of discomfort. Do not push on.

## What should we expect from the driver?

A good driver on a ghat road is calm, steady and unhurried. Expect the following and feel free to ask for them:

- A **steady pace**, particularly on descent.
- **Breaks** when passengers need them.
- Giving way where the road is narrow. Traditionally, vehicles climbing are given priority on tight bends; the driver will know the local practice.
- **No overtaking on blind curves.**

If you ever feel the pace is too fast, say so politely. A good operator wants to hear it.

## What about the descent?

Going down is harder on the brakes and sometimes harder on passengers. Allow time, leave while there is light and avoid pushing for the "one last view". If mist builds, go down earlier rather than later. Our [one-day plan from Dindigul](/blog/one-day-kodaikanal-trip-from-dindigul) builds in a daylight return.

## Should we avoid night driving on the ghat?

If you can, yes. Visibility drops, fog is more likely, wildlife and other vehicles are harder to see, and passengers are more tired. If you must travel after dark, tell the operator in advance so it can be planned for.

## What about rain, mist and landslides?

Heavy rain can make the road slippery, and landslides sometimes affect hill roads in the monsoon. Check the forecast and local advisories on the day. If the route is disrupted, change the plan; no sightseeing day is worth a risky drive. Mist, even without rain, slows everything down.

## Why does a single vehicle help?

On a hairpin road, a convoy of cars is awkward: someone falls behind, someone overtakes, someone stops in a bad place. A single vehicle moves at one pace, stops once and arrives together. It also keeps the group's belongings and medicines in one place. ## What should I tell the operator about the group?

Mention:

- Children, elders and anyone prone to motion sickness.
- Whether you plan to return after dark.
- Whether you want the driver to stop at viewpoints on the way.
- Any health conditions that affect pace.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Kodaikanal ghat road dangerous?
It is a hill road with hairpin bends that needs a steady driver and good weather. Plan breaks, avoid night and heavy rain, and do not rush.

### Is there fuel on the ghat road?
Public sources say there is none on the ghat stretch, so fill up before you climb.

### How do I stop children getting sick on the climb?
Seat them near the front, keep the air fresh, avoid screens, give any doctor-approved remedy beforehand and take breaks.

### Should we avoid the road in the monsoon?
Check the forecast and local advisories. Heavy rain and mist slow the drive and can disrupt the road.
