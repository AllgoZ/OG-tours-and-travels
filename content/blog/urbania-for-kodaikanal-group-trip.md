## Why is a single vehicle better for a Kodaikanal group?

A hill station is the place where splitting a group across several cars costs you most:

- **The ghat road.** Following a convoy up a winding hill road in mist is stressful for drivers and passengers. One vehicle removes the coordination.
- **Parking.** At peak times parking near the lake and viewpoints is limited. One vehicle needs one space, not three.
- **Meeting points.** With one vehicle there is only one place where the group meets after each stop.
- **Luggage.** Jackets, snacks, a change of clothes for the children and bags for an overnight stay stay together.
- **Schedule.** One decision, not three, about when to leave a viewpoint.

## Which Kodaikanal groups benefit most?

- **Joint families and multi-generation groups,** where older and younger members need a gentler pace.
- **Friends' groups** who want to travel and talk together.
- **Celebration trips,** such as a family reunion or a small function that ends with a hill-station weekend.

## What should I confirm in a private Urbania booking?

A private booking means the vehicle is arranged for your group alone. Before you confirm, get answers in writing to:

1. **Seating and luggage space** for your exact group.
2. **What the quotation includes** and what is extra (tolls, parking, night halt, waiting time).
3. **Pickup and drop times**, and your return plan if you stay overnight.
4. **Driver and contact details** the day before.
5. **How weather or route changes are handled** — hill roads sometimes need an adjusted plan.
6. **Advance and cancellation terms.**

## How does a Kodaikanal day with a private vehicle work in practice?

You agree the pickup point and time beforehand and everyone boards together. The vehicle stays with your group through the day: it carries your jackets, snacks and bags while you walk around the lake or a viewpoint, and it is the fixed place where the group meets again. If the mist comes down and you want to swap the order of the day, one conversation changes the plan for everyone. At the end of the day you leave together, instead of waiting for the last car to find parking. Specific arrangements — where the vehicle waits, how the driver's breaks work, what happens on a night halt — should be agreed in your quotation.

## What are the limits of a single vehicle?

Being honest about this helps you decide:

- **Very large groups** may need more than one vehicle.
- **Very small groups** may find a car simpler.
- **Different itineraries** — if half the group wants to go somewhere else for the day, one vehicle cannot be in two places.
- **Narrow lanes and tight parking** in a hill town mean the driver may drop you at a convenient point and park elsewhere; agree the arrangement beforehand.

Knowing these before you book is better than discovering them at the lake.
