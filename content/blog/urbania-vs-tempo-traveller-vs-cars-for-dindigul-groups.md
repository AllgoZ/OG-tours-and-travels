"Should we take a Tempo Traveller, an Urbania or just two cars?" is one of the most common questions a group organiser asks, and the honest answer is: it depends on the group. Marketing for each option is full of adjectives; very little of it helps you decide. This article compares the **Force Urbania, a Tempo Traveller-style van and a set of cars** on the things that actually matter on a trip from Dindigul. We offer the **Force Urbania** at **OG Tours and Travels**, so we say upfront where it is the right choice and where it is not.

*We do not publish seat counts, features or prices for any vehicle here. Configurations differ between vehicles and operators, so confirm specifics for your booking in writing.*

## What are we comparing?

- **Force Urbania** — a large passenger van from Force Motors designed for carrying groups on long journeys.
- **Tempo Traveller-style van** — the widely used passenger van category for group trips. Force Motors also makes the Traveller; other makes exist.
- **Cars** — two or more private cars or taxis for a group that splits up.

The Urbania and the Traveller are neighbours in the group-van segment. Before deciding, ask the operator for the exact vehicle and seating you will get, rather than relying on the model name.

## How do they compare for a family or group?

| Question | Urbania / group van | Several cars |
|---|---|---|
| Does the group travel together? | Yes, in one vehicle | Split across vehicles |
| One pickup and one drop? | Yes | Several |
| Convoy to keep together? | No | Yes |
| Good for 2–4 people? | Usually more than needed | Usually the better fit |
| Luggage for a large group | Shared space in one vehicle | Divided between cars |
| Flexibility for different routes | One plan for everyone | Each car can differ |
| Parking in hill towns | One vehicle to park | Several |

## How do I decide on group size?

Count honestly. If you are **two to four people**, a car is simpler and usually cheaper. If you are **five or six**, you are in between: compare quotes. If you are **a large family or a team**, a group van keeps everyone together. If your group is **larger than any single vehicle**, you need more than one. Our guide to [choosing the right vehicle for group travel](/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-vehicle-for-group-travel) takes you through this step by step.

## How do they compare on hill roads?

On a ghat road like the one to Kodaikanal, the main factors are not the model name:

- **A steady, experienced driver** matters more than the badge.
- **Seating position** — those prone to motion sickness do better near the front.
- **Space to rest**, so that children and older members can lie back or move.
- **One vehicle** on the hairpins rather than a convoy.

See our [Kodaikanal ghat road tips](/blog/kodaikanal-ghat-road-tips-for-group-travel) and [why choose Urbania for a Kodaikanal group trip](/blog/urbania-for-kodaikanal-group-trip).

## What about luggage?

Ask for the luggage plan, not only the seat count. A hill-station weekend with suitcases is different from a day trip with a couple of bags. If you carry large items, tell the operator in your first message so that the vehicle and the seating can be matched.

## What about comfort on long drives?

Comfort is subjective, and we avoid claiming that one model is better than another in general. Instead, ask:

1. How much **legroom and headroom** is there in the seats your group will use?
2. How is the **ventilation**, and can windows open for those who need air?
3. Are there **seat-belts, charging points** or other features you care about?
4. What is the **luggage space**?

Ask for photos of the actual vehicle, and ask the operator to confirm anything that matters to you.

## When is a Force Urbania the better choice?

- A **family or team** that wants to stay together on a long outstation trip.
- A trip with **hill roads**, where a single vehicle avoids convoy problems.
- A **group with luggage** that is easier to carry in one place.
- Anyone who values **one point of contact**.

## When is it not?

- **Two or three people** — take a car.
- **A very large group** — you may need more than one vehicle.
- **A tight budget with flexible people** — public transport or a car may be right.
