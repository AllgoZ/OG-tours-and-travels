Palani and Kodaikanal sit in the same range of hills, which is why so many families treat them as one plan: a morning of darshan in the temple town, and a day or two of cool air and viewpoints up the hill. A **Palani to Kodaikanal trip** is also one of the shorter hill drives in the region — public sources put it at roughly 65–70 km — but it is still a ghat road, and the way you plan the two halves matters. This guide covers the route, a realistic schedule, what to pack and how to keep a family comfortable, including in a **Force Urbania** booked through **OG Tours and Travels**.

*Distances are approximate and sources differ; check your route on a map. We have not quoted journey times because they depend heavily on the day, the traffic and the weather.*

## How far is Kodaikanal from Palani?

Roughly **65 to 70 km** by road, according to public travel sources. Buses and cars from Palani commonly go via the hill road through places such as Perumalmalai, though you should confirm the current route and road conditions on a map before you leave. It is the shortest of the three common routes to Kodaikanal from this region, but most of it is climbing, so allow more time than the figure suggests.

## Can we do Palani and Kodaikanal in one day?

It is possible, but we would not plan it that way for a family. Palani temple visits can run long — queues, security checks, climbing or the other ways up the hill — and Kodaikanal needs a start in the morning to be enjoyable. Most families find this approach more comfortable:

- **Day one:** Palani temple in the morning, an easy afternoon, rest.
- **Day two:** a morning start for Kodaikanal, sightseeing, and then either stay the night or return in daylight.

If you only have one day, give the whole day to one place. Rushing both leaves everyone tired.

## What should we plan around in Palani?

- **Go early.** Mornings are cooler and queues are usually shorter.
- **Expect crowds** on weekends and during festival periods, when roads fill with walking pilgrims. Allow extra time.
- **Check temple timings and dress rules** before you leave and tell everyone in the group.
- **Decide who climbs and who waits.** Agree a meeting point, so the group is not scattered.

## What should we plan around in Kodaikanal?

Kodaikanal's main sights are close together: **Kodai Lake**, **Coaker's Walk**, **Pillar Rocks**, **Guna Caves**, **Green Valley View**, **Bryant Park** and the **Silver Cascade**. For children and older members, choose three or four rather than all of them. ## What should we carry for the family?

- Light **jackets or sweaters** for everyone, children included — Kodaikanal is cooler and can be misty.
- **Temple-appropriate clothes** for the Palani leg, kept easily accessible.
- **Water, snacks, wet wipes and plastic bags.**
- **Motion-sickness remedies** and spare clothes for small children.
- A small **umbrella or raincoat**.
- **ID proof** and a phone with enough charge.

## Hill-road tips for families

- **Avoid the ghat in heavy rain, mist or after dark** when you can.
- **Take a break** if someone feels unwell rather than pushing on.
- **Seat those prone to motion sickness near the front.**
- **Give yourselves a buffer** on weekends, when town traffic slows.

## What does a two-day Palani and Kodaikanal plan look like?

A relaxed plan for a family of mixed ages:

**Day one — Palani**
1. An early start to the temple, so you are among the first in the queue rather than the last.
2. Agree a meeting point and time for the group after the darshan.
3. Lunch and a rest in the heat of the afternoon.
4. If you are staying in Palani, an easy evening. If you want to drive towards Kodaikanal that night, think twice: a hill road after a tiring day is the wrong combination.

**Day two — Kodaikanal**
1. Leave early for the hill station.
2. Take a break on the climb — water, fresh air, a restroom stop.
3. Start with Kodai Lake, then pick one or two of the other sights.
4. Lunch in town, a short rest, and an afternoon stop only if everyone still has energy.
5. Begin the return in good time to be off the ghat in daylight.

## How can we make the temple day easier for older relatives?

- **Talk about the plan beforehand.** Who will climb, who will use other ways up, and where does the group meet afterwards?
- **Choose the quietest slot you can**, even if it means an earlier start.
- **Keep footwear, water and a change of clothes accessible** rather than buried in the bags.
- **Do not plan anything immediately after the darshan.** Leave a buffer; temple days run longer than expected.

Travelling in one vehicle helps because the group can leave together and the vehicle is the fixed meeting point.

## What if we are short of time?

If you have only one day and want both, be realistic about what "both" means. A short, early temple visit followed by a brief Kodaikanal stop — one lakeside walk, one viewpoint — can work for a fit group, but it is a long day and the return drive will be after a tiring day. If anyone in the group is elderly, very young or prone to motion sickness, we suggest choosing one place and doing it well. Many families return for the other destination on another weekend, which is far more enjoyable than a rushed double.

## What if the weather turns bad?

Hill-station weather changes quickly. If you reach the hill road in heavy rain or thick mist, there is nothing wrong with delaying the climb, changing the day or shortening the sightseeing. Check the forecast the evening before, keep a flexible plan and decide in the morning. A missed viewpoint is a small price compared with an unsafe drive.
