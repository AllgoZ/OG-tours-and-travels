Travelling with a big group is one of the best ways to share a journey — but more people means more to coordinate. With a little organisation, a larger group trip can be just as relaxed as a small one. Here is how to keep everyone comfortable and on schedule.

## Keep the group together in one vehicle

The simplest way to reduce stress is to travel together. When everyone is in one vehicle:

- Nobody gets separated or lost in convoy.
- Stops happen together, so no one is left waiting.
- The journey itself becomes part of the trip, not a logistics exercise.

For a larger group, this single decision removes most of the usual headaches.

## Plan seating and luggage in advance

With more passengers, space needs a little thought:

- Consider who travels best where — those prone to motion sickness up front, children where they can be supervised.
- Keep **everyday bags** accessible and stow the rest.
- Be realistic about luggage. Confirm there is room for your group's bags before the day.

If you are unsure whether your group and luggage will fit comfortably, ask before booking and share your numbers — it is better to confirm than to assume.

## Build stops into the schedule

A bigger group simply takes longer at each stop. Rather than fighting it, plan for it:

- Allow a little extra time at each break.
- Choose stops with enough facilities for everyone.
- Agree a rough "back on the road" time at each stop so nobody wanders off.

> With a large group, generous timing is the difference between a relaxed trip and a rushed one.

## Communicate before and during

- Share the plan — route, stops and timings — with everyone beforehand.
- Nominate one person as the point of contact for the group.
- Keep everyone's numbers saved and shared.

## Keep everyone comfortable

Comfort scales with the group. Keep water and snacks within reach, manage the air-conditioning for everyone, and do not overload the day's plan. A relaxed pace keeps a big group happy far better than an ambitious itinerary.

## A quick large-group checklist

- One vehicle with comfortable space for the whole group.
- Seating and luggage planned in advance.
- Stops and timings agreed and shared.
- A single point of contact for the group.
