Transport is one of those parts of an event that nobody notices when it goes well — and everybody notices when it does not. Whether it is a wedding, a family function or a corporate event, smooth group transport comes down to timing and clear communication. Here is how to approach it.

## Start with the schedule

Event transport is built around the event timeline, so begin there:

- When and where does each part of the day happen?
- When do guests need to **arrive**, and when will they **leave**?
- Are there multiple venues, or a single location?

Work backwards from the key moments. If guests must be seated by a certain time, plan pickups with enough buffer for traffic and the inevitable last-minute delays.

## Map your pickup points

Rather than collecting everyone individually, group guests around a few sensible pickup points:

- A central hotel or two where out-of-town guests are staying.
- A well-known landmark that is easy to find.
- The homes of key family members, if that suits a smaller function.

Fewer, clearer pickup points are far easier to coordinate than many scattered ones.

## Communicate clearly with guests

Most transport problems are really communication problems. Make sure guests know:

- **Where** to be and **what time** to be there.
- Who to contact if they are running late or cannot find the vehicle.
- Any return-journey arrangements.

> A simple message with the pickup point, time and a contact number prevents most of the day's confusion.

## Share the details early for an accurate plan

When you enquire about event transport, share the full picture — dates, timings, venues, pickup points and the number of passengers. The more complete your brief, the more accurate the arrangement and quotation will be. For events, confirming these details well in advance is especially important, since timings are fixed and there is little room to improvise on the day.

## A short event-transport checklist

- Event timeline mapped, with buffer time built in.
- Pickup points chosen and shared with guests.
- Passenger numbers confirmed.
- A contact person for the day agreed.
- Return journeys planned, not left to chance.
